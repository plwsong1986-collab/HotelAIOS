# Purpose

This document defines how Sanlitian tells stories.

Unlike the Brand Story, which explains the origin of the brand, this document establishes the principles, structure, and narrative approach used when creating stories across all communication channels.

It serves as the **Single Source of Truth (SSOT)** for storytelling within HotelAIOS.

---

# Why Stories Matter

People rarely remember a list of features.

They remember how a place made them feel.

At Sanlitian, storytelling helps us share not only where guests stay, but why their experience is meaningful.

Every story should help readers feel closer to Lijiang, its people, and its slower rhythm of life.

---

# What We Tell Stories About

Our stories always begin with people, places, and experiences.

We tell stories about:

- Guests
- Local residents
- The Three-Eyed Well
- Lijiang Ancient Town
- Yunnan culture
- Tea
- Coffee
- Nature
- Everyday life
- Quiet moments
- Small discoveries

Rooms and facilities support the story.

They are not the story.

---

# What We Do Not Tell Stories About

Avoid stories built around promotion instead of experience.

Do not create content focused on:

- Discounts
- Luxury
- Competition
- "Best Hotel" claims
- Sales language
- Artificial excitement

If a story could describe any hotel,

it probably should not describe Sanlitian.

---

# Story Structure

Most stories should follow a simple narrative structure.

## 1. Begin with a Moment

Start with a real scene.

Examples include:

- Morning sunlight entering the courtyard
- Steam rising from a cup of Yunnan tea
- A quiet walk beside the Three-Eyed Well
- Fresh coffee brewed before exploring Lijiang

Invite readers into the experience.

---

## 2. Explain Why It Matters

Connect the moment to something meaningful.

For example:

A cup of tea becomes a conversation.

A morning walk becomes a discovery.

A peaceful night's sleep becomes the beginning of a better journey.

Help readers understand why the experience matters.

---

## 3. End with an Invitation

Instead of asking people to book a room,

invite them to experience Lijiang differently.

Allow the story to continue in the reader's imagination.

---

# Describing Experiences

Describe what guests feel rather than what they receive.

Instead of:

> Spacious room with oxygen equipment.

Prefer:

> Wake up refreshed after a peaceful night and begin exploring Lijiang with renewed energy.

---

Instead of:

> Complimentary tea.

Prefer:

> Spend a quiet afternoon sharing a cup of Yunnan tea in the courtyard.

---

Instead of:

> Convenient location.

Prefer:

> Step outside and begin your morning among the stone streets of Lijiang Ancient Town.

Focus on experiences before amenities.

---

# Writing Principles

Every story should be:

- Honest
- Calm
- Human
- Simple
- Visual
- Authentic
- Easy to understand

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

Choose specific details instead of general praise.

Show rather than tell.

---

# Story Sources

Many meaningful stories grow from ordinary moments.

Examples include:

- Preparing breakfast
- Brewing coffee
- Making tea
- Walking to Zhongyi Market
- Talking with returning guests
- Watching the sunrise
- Rain in the courtyard
- Seasonal flowers
- Traditional festivals
- Everyday conversations

Small moments often become memorable stories.

---

# Storytelling Across Channels

The same story may appear in different formats while preserving its core meaning.

**Website**

A complete narrative with context and detail.

---

**Social Media**

A single memorable moment.

---

**AI Assistant**

A helpful explanation told with warmth.

---

**Email**

A personal recommendation.

---

**Video**

Visual storytelling supported by simple narration.

The format may change.

The heart of the story should remain the same.

---

# One Simple Rule

Never ask:

> What should we promote today?

Always ask:

> What meaningful experience can we share today?

That is where every Sanlitian story begins.
