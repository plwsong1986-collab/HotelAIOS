# Purpose

This document is the authoritative source for the organizational architecture of culture-related knowledge within the Culture Domain.

It defines how cultural information is structured, classified, and related to other knowledge domains. The document supports AI assistants, semantic search, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), ontology development, and knowledge management through a Single Source of Truth (SSOT).

---

# Overview

The Culture Domain organizes destination cultural knowledge into distinct but related categories.

Each category represents a unique cultural perspective while maintaining clear ownership boundaries. The architecture is designed to maximize semantic consistency, minimize knowledge duplication, and improve AI retrieval.

This document defines the architecture of the Culture Domain only. It does not define the detailed content of individual cultural topics.

# Core Information

## Domain Structure

The Culture Domain consists of the following knowledge categories:

- History
- Ethnic Culture
- Local Customs
- Festivals
- Performing Arts
- Traditional Crafts
- Tea Culture
- Local Language

Each category owns a distinct area of cultural knowledge while contributing to the overall understanding of the destination.

---

## Architectural Principles

The Culture Domain architecture follows these principles:

- Clear topic separation
- Explicit ownership boundaries
- Consistent terminology
- Semantic organization
- Modular knowledge structure
- AI-friendly organization
- Ontology compatibility

Each document should remain independent while supporting cross-domain relationships.

---

## Information Hierarchy

The Culture Domain organizes knowledge at multiple levels:

**Domain Level**

Defines the overall scope and ownership of culture-related knowledge.

**Category Level**

Organizes knowledge into individual cultural subjects.

**Topic Level**

Describes specific cultural concepts, traditions, terminology, or practices within each category.

This hierarchy supports scalable documentation and efficient semantic retrieval.
